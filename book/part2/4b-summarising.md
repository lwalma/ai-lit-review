# Summarising

If you are reading a source that is highly relevant for your project, a summary can help you use the source in the future. While you may have already done some note-taking while close reading your sources, a summary differs in that it is usually a bit more structured and helps you recognise the key points of a source later. Summarising a source can also help you understand and evaluate a source better. 

It might be tempting to upload a research article into an AI tool and ask for a direct summary of the source. However, it is often better to rely on the **abstract of a source** as an initial summary and add your own thoughts, rather than having AI provide the summary for you. In academic articles, the abstract is provided by the authors and checked by the editors and reviewers. It is therefore more reliable than an AI summary, as AI can get parts of paper completely or partly wrong, or provide summaries only based on parts of an article (often the title and abstract). Moreover, by reading, summarising and synthesising the information yourself, you contribute to your own learning process, and you will miss out on this if you rely on AI to do this for you. Finally, it is good to realise that research articles, while usually available to students through the university or open access, are copyright protected. It is often not allowed to upload these articles to AI tools, as it is not always clear where the information will end up.

## Recap Standard Practice: Identify basic components of a source and choose a summary format

**Identify Basic Components**
When creating a summary, you can choose from a wide variety of formats, but there are some basic components that should always be included:
- Bibliographical information. For example, author, publication, year. All the details you need to include to relocate the source later in your project. This can also help you when referencing.
- Topic / Research Question of the source
- Methods used
- Main results
- Connection to previous studies
- Limitations of the study
- Implications of the findings

**Selecting a Format**
Summaries can come in many formats. Depending on your purpose they can be more or less elaborate, depending on how detailed you need to summarise and analyse your source.

Example formats
- Mindmaps
- Literature Matrix
- Short Summary
- Visual Tools (Miro, Powerpoint or Canva)
- Annotated Bibliography

If you want to learn more about this standard practice, have a look at **__TULIB PAGE__**

## AI Assist 1: Have AI check if you have all basic components for your sources
Before you start summarising you sources, it can be helpful to check if you have all the basic information available. Especially if you have a lot of unstructured notes, asking a general AI tool to check if the information is included can be helpful.

```{admonition} Example Prompt
:class: dropdown note
You are an expert reviewer of scientific literature on [topic]. Check notes in the attached file if the following components are included for each source: bibliographical information, topic or research question, methods used, main results, connection to previous studies, limitations of the study, implications of the findings. List the sources where the information is missing in a table, describing in 1-2 sentences what information is missing
```

```{warning}
Be careful and don't upload sensitive of copyrighted data to AI tools, like academic journal articles. When in doubt contact the <a href="https://www.tudelft.nl/library/support/copyright" target="_blank">Copyright Information Point</a>
```

## AI Assist 2: Have AI organise your notes into a preferred summary format
Once you have made sure that you have all the necessary components for the sources you want to analyse, you can use AI to support you to summarise your sources in a helpful format. For this you could use a tool like NotebookLM, or a general AI tool (Subwerkgroep AI Literacy, 2026). You can use these summaries as overviews so you can remember later what the sources are about, to dive deeper in the text and check your understanding, or you can build on the summary further, adding more of your own thoughts and analyses to get ready to synthesise your sources.

```{admonition} Example Prompt for a General AI Tool
:class: dropdown note
You are an expert scientific writer with an expertise for writing effective summaries. Based on the attached notes generate a short textual summary of 200-300 words, including the following: bibliographical information, topic or research question, methods used, main results, connection to previous studies, limitations of the study, and implications of the findings.
```

Alternatively, you could use multimedia summaries. These can be helpful if you want to engage with your sources in a different manner. Google's NotebookLM is a local-input tool that provides a lot of ways to engage with sources. Based on the sources you put in, you can ask NotebookLM to provide you with a textual summary, or in other formats, like a podcasts or a video. You could use these summaries of your notes to further engage with your texts and guide your understanding.

```{admonition} Example Prompt for NotebookLM
:class: dropdown note 
Have a look at the relevant parts of <a href="https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=FOs4RDTC52Q" target=_blank>this video by Teacher's Tech</a> if you want to learn how to:
- Generate an <a href="https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=FOs4RDTC52Q&t=538s" target="_blank">audio summary</a> 
- Generate a <a href="https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=FOs4RDTC52Q&t=668s" target="_blank">video summary</a>
```

```{admonition} Guided Activity: Turn Your Notes into Summaries
:class: dropdown tip

**Step 1: Choose a Tool**
Work with NotebookLM and one general AI tool

**Step 2: Upload Your Notes**  
- Choose relevant sources from your reading list.  
- Upload your notes on the sources into a local-input AI tool and into NotebookLM.  

**Step 3: Request a Textual Summary**  
- Ask the general AI tool to provide a short summary of the article based on your notes. You can use the following example prompt for this:
You are an expert scientific writer with an expertise for writing effective summaries. Based on the attached notes generate a short textual summary of 200-300 words, including the following: bibliographical information, topic or research question, methods used, main results, connection to previous studies, limitations of the study, and implications of the findings.

**Step 4: Use NotebookLM to create a multimedia summary**
- Pick one format you would like to try out (video, audio, powerpoint)
- Upload your notes into NotebookLM and ask it to create a multimedia summary
Follow along with <a href="https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=FOs4RDTC52Q" target=_blank>the video by Teacher's Tech</a> to:  
- Generate an <a href="https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=FOs4RDTC52Q&t=538s" target="_blank">audio summary</a> 
- Generate a <a href="https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=FOs4RDTC52Q&t=668s" target="_blank">video summary</a>

**Step 5: Verify With Your Own Reading**  
- Go through the textual and multimedia summary carefully
- Compare the AI-generated summary to your own understanding of the article and the actual content.  
- Highlight areas where the summary was accurate, incomplete, or misleading. 

**Step 6: Reflect on the Outcomes**  
- How well did the AI tools capture the main points written down in your notes?
- Did the audio or video summary help you approach the source more easily?
- Which of the summary types was most helpful for your understanding?  
```
