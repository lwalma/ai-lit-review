# Summarising

If you are reading a source that is highly relevant for your project, a summary can help you use the source in the future. While you may have already done some note-taking while close reading your sources, a summary differs in that it is usually a bit more structured and helps you recognise the key points of a source later. Summarising a source can also help you understand and evaluate a source better.

## Recap Standard Practice: Identify basic components of a source and choose a summary format

**Identify Basic Components**
When creating a summary, you can choose from a wide variety of formats, but there are some basic components that should always be included:
- Bibliographical information. For example, author, publication, year. All the details you need to include to relocate the source later in your project. This can also help you when referencing.
- Topic / Research Question of the source
- Methods used
- Main results
- Connection to previous studies
- Limitations of the study
- Implications of the findings

**Selecting a Format**
Summaries can come in many formats. Depending on your purpose they can be more or less elaborate, depending on how detailed you need to summarise and analyse your source.

Example formats
- Mindmaps
- Literature Matrix
- Short Summary
- Visual Tools (Miro, Powerpoint or Canva)
- Annotated Bibliography

If you want to learn more about this standard practice, have a look at **__TULIB PAGE__**

## AI Assist 2: Have AI organise your basic components into a preferred summary format
Once you have made sure that you have all the necessary components for the sources you want to analyse, you can use AI to support you to summarise your sources in a helpful format. For this you could use a tool like NotebookLM, or a general AI tool.

While these tools can save time, AI can get parts of a paper completely or partly wrong. Tools misinterpret papers and cannot be trusted to do this correctly. It is often better to rely on the abstract of a source as an initial summary, rather than having AI provide the summary for you. 

We therefore recommend that you do the reading and notetaking yourself, and use AI to support your reading, not as a replacement. Moreover, by reading, summarising and synthesising the information yourself, you contribute to your own learning process, and you will miss out on this if you rely on AI to do this for you.

```{warning}
Be careful and don't upload sensitive of copyrighted data to AI tools, when in doubt contact the <a href="https://www.tudelft.nl/library/support/copyright" target="_blank">Copyright Information Point</a>
```
