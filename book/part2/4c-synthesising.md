# Synthesising Findings

Next step in the analysis is synthesising, where you compare the different information you have found and connect your findings to your research question.  The key question in a synthesis is often: how do the resources you found and read relate to one another, in your interpretation and what variety of answers have you found for your information search questions?

## Recap Standard Practice: Organise Your Findings and Compare Sources

Based on your observations, group your sources in a way that makes sense for the question you want to answer or the argument you want to make. When grouping your sources, you integrate the different sources to tell your perspective on the literature you found. 

Rather than a summary, this is where you combine the sources to create, for example, a new overview of the literature on a topic (e.g., introduction or literature review) or combine the main findings in the sources with your own results to discuss your findings (e.g., discussion). 

What to focus on is related to your research question. As you go comparing your sources and adding your own voice, decide what the important themes and answers you need from the literature are. Some examples of how you can group your sources include:
- Discuss methods used in your field
- Analyse important topics
- Showcase discussions in the field
- Present a chronological progression of a topic

After deciding what way you want to group and compare your sources, and what specific topics you are going to look for in the literature, you can start comparing the information in your sources. A helpful tool can be a synthesis matrix. In a synthesis matrix you list the sources you have selected for your project and compare the findings on topics you have selected. It could look like this:

| Sub-topic or theme | Source 1 | Source 2 | Source 3 | My Conclusions |
|-|-|-|-|-|
| Sub-topic or theme 1 | relevant information from source 1 about theme 1 | relevant information from source 2 about theme 1 | relevant information from source 3 about theme 1 |  ... |
| Sub-topic or theme 2 | relevant information from source 1 about theme 2 | ... | ... | ... |
| Sub-topic or theme 3 | relevant information from source 1 about theme 3 | ... | ... | ... |

The process of synthesising is a key point where learning takes place and you should not outsource this part to AI tools. One practical way to think about AI and learning is to distinguish between useful friction and unnecessary friction. Useful friction is effort that contributes to learning. Just as muscles develop through physical effort, you develop knowledge, skills and judgement by working through meaningful challenges (Teaching and Learning Services, TU Delft, 2026). If too much of that effort is taken over by AI, your literature review may still look good, but you will miss the learning process that gives the result its value. The skills needed for synthesising (and reading and summarising) are examples of this useful friction. When you are reading, summarising and synthesizing your findings, you practice the thinking and decision-making skills you need for your future career. In addition, by working through this useful friction you become more of an expert in your topic.

## AI Assist 1: Have AI Draft Your Literature Matrix Based on Your Notes, Summaries and Themes

AI can support the development of your synthesis matrix, based on the notes you made while you were reviewing your sources and your initial ideas for themes you want to address in your literature review. You can ask AI to put the information from your own notes in a draft synthesis matrix for you to work on further. 

You will get the best results if you have made sure that your summaries are in a consistent format and no information is missing. See also section [4b: Summarising](4b-summarising.md).

```{admonition} Example Prompt
:class: dropdown note
You are an expert reviewer of scientific literature on [topic]. Create a synthesis matrix based on the research notes pasted below. Use the following themes as rows [theme 1, theme 2, theme 3] and the source titles as columns. For each theme, list if it is covered or not, and if it is covered, and what it says about the theme. Create a final column called "my conclusions" and leave this blank.<br>
[attach or paste notes].
```

```{admonition} Guided Activity: Have AI Draft Your Literature Matrix Based on Your Notes, Summaries and Themes
:class: dropdown note
**Step 1.** Choose a general AI tool, for example Copilot, ChatGPT or Claude. Or you could work with NotebookLM.

**Step 2.** Select the notes, summaries and themes you would like the AI to put into a literature matrix.

**Step 3.** Ask the AI to create the synthesis matrix. You can use the following prompt (and adjust it where necessary):

_You are an expert reviewer of scientific literature on [topic]. Create a synthesis matrix based on the research notes pasted below. Use the following themes as rows [theme 1, theme 2, theme 3] and the source titles as columns. For each theme, list if it is covered or not, and if it is covered, and what it says about the theme. Create a final column called "my conclusions" and leave this blank. <br>
[attach or paste notes]_

**Step 4.** Evaluate the outcome:
- Is the literature matrix useful?
- Has the AI tool included the correct information from your notes?
- How could you build further on this matrix?
```

## AI Assist 2: Use AI as a Sparring Partner to Check Your Initial Conclusions
You could use a general AI tool to check your initial conclusions and see if there is anything missing. Rather than copying the suggested improvements from the AI, we recommend you use the tool as a sparring partner to ask critical questions and help furher shape your conclusions. Be reminded that AI tools don't think: they produce content based on prompts they receive and databases they are trained with. The feedback the tool gives can therefore be helpful, but your own ideas and analysis should be in the lead.

```{admonition} Example Prompt
:class: dropdown note
You are an expert reviewer of scientific literature on [topic]. You are writing a literature review with the following research question: [research question]. Below are the preliminary conclusions, based on the attached [notes or synthesis matrix]. Check the conclusions for consistency with the research question and the input on the notes. List any potential issues with the conclusions in a bulleted list. <br>
[attach or paste notes, matrix or conclusions]
```

```{admonition} Guided Activity: Use AI as a Sparring Partner to Check Your Initial Conclusions
:class: dropdown note
**Step 1.** Choose a general AI tool, for example Copilot, ChatGPT or Claude. Or you could work with NotebookLM.

**Step 2.** Select the draft conclusion you would like feedback on and your accompanying literature matrix or research notes.

**Step 3.** Ask the AI to evaluate your conclusions. You can use the following prompt (and adjust it where necessary):

_You are an expert reviewer of scientific literature on [topic]. You are writing a literature review with the following research question: [research question]. Below are the preliminary conclusions, based on the attached [notes or synthesis matrix]. Check the conclusions for consistency with the research question and the input on the notes. List any potential issues with the conclusions in a bulleted list, including 1-2 sentences on why there is a issue with the conclusion.
[attach or paste notes, matrix or conclusions]_

**Step 4.** Evaluate the outcome:
- Do you agree with the feedback given by the AI?
- How can you use this feedback to further develop your conclusions?
```
